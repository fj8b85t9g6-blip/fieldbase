# FieldBase Low-Voltage Contractor Sales Kit

## Ideal customer profile

Start with owner-led low-voltage contractors that have roughly 2-25 field technicians and coordinate work across direct clients, email or phone dispatches, and one or more marketplaces. Strong-fit trades include structured cabling, network installation, CCTV, access control, commercial AV, and service-call work.

Prioritize teams where the owner or dispatcher still rebuilds the schedule by hand, confirms assignments through text messages, and creates invoices after the field work is complete. Deprioritize companies that require a certified integration, workflow, or compliance control FieldBase cannot demonstrate today.

## Positioning

**One-line value proposition**

FieldBase gives a low-voltage contractor one place to capture jobs, spot schedule conflicts, assign technicians, document completion, and move completed work toward invoicing.

**Thirty-second talk track**

"FieldBase is built for small field-service crews that are coordinating jobs from calls, screenshots, marketplaces, and direct clients. You can capture a job by voice or screenshot, review the details, put it on the calendar, assign a tech, and keep the closeout and invoice workflow connected. The goal is fewer handoffs for the owner and a clearer workday for the crew."

**Proof rule**

Only claim what can be shown in the current environment. Before outreach, verify login, job creation, calendar conflict behavior, team assignment, closeout, invoice generation, payment-link configuration, screenshot extraction, voice capture, and any marketplace sync used in the demo. Describe unavailable or unconfigured features as roadmap or setup items, never as live capabilities.

## Buyer and trigger map

| Buyer | What they care about | Useful trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Owner/operator | Margin visibility, fewer missed jobs, faster admin | Hiring a first dispatcher or adding technicians |
| Dispatcher/project coordinator | Clean schedule, assignment clarity, fewer text threads | Double-booking or growing service volume |
| Office manager/bookkeeper | Complete job records and timely invoice handoff | Invoice backlog or missing closeout details |
| Lead technician | Clear scope, address, timing, and deliverables | Repeated calls for missing job information |

## Discovery call

Open with: "I want to understand how a job moves from the first call to technician assignment and then to an invoice. Could you walk me through the last service call you completed?"

Ask:

1. Where do new jobs arrive today?
2. Who enters each job into the schedule, and what gets copied by hand?
3. How do you catch a double-booking before a technician is committed?
4. What information is most often missing when a technician arrives?
5. How is assignment acceptance confirmed?
6. What must come back from the site before the job can be closed?
7. Who creates the invoice, and how long after completion does that happen?
8. Which workflow must remain unchanged for a pilot to be acceptable?
9. Which systems or marketplace connections are mandatory?
10. What would make a 14-day pilot clearly worth continuing?

Qualify a pilot only when there is a named owner, a repeatable job type, three to ten real jobs suitable for testing, and an agreed success measure. Stop the process if a required integration or control cannot be demonstrated.

## Demo script

Use a synthetic job with a fictional customer and address.

1. Open New Job on a phone and dictate a structured-cabling service call.
2. Stop recording, review the transcript, and extract the job details.
3. Correct one field to show that the owner remains in control of AI-created data.
4. Assign a technician and place the job on the calendar.
5. Create a second overlapping job and show the actual conflict behavior available in the app.
6. Open the technician-facing workflow and show only the information verified in that session.
7. Complete the sample job and demonstrate the configured closeout and invoice path.
8. End with the prospect's workflow: "Which step here would remove the most back-and-forth for your team?"

Do not use an unverified sync or payment path as the demo climax. Keep a manual job-entry path ready so a provider outage does not end the conversation.

## Pilot offer

**Scope:** One dispatcher or owner, up to five technicians, and one repeatable job type for 14 days.

**Included:** Kickoff, configuration review, team onboarding, synthetic test job, real-job pilot, day-three check-in, and day-fourteen review.

**Customer responsibilities:** Provide an internal owner, choose eligible jobs, confirm what customer data may be entered, and report issues with the job ID and time observed.

**Success measures:** Fill these in during discovery rather than promising an outcome.

- Percentage of pilot jobs captured in FieldBase: `[target]`
- Median time from intake to technician assignment: `[baseline]` to `[target]`
- Pilot jobs with complete site details before dispatch: `[target]`
- Completed pilot jobs ready for invoice review the same day: `[target]`
- Owner/dispatcher weekly admin time: `[baseline]` to `[target]`

## Onboarding runbook

### Before kickoff

- Name the account owner and backup contact.
- Confirm technician names, roles, and email addresses.
- Select one job type and three to ten pilot jobs.
- Document required fields, closeout evidence, and assignment rules.
- Verify every integration that will be mentioned during onboarding.
- Agree on customer-data handling and who may access the account.

### Kickoff (45 minutes)

- Reconfirm the current intake-to-invoice workflow.
- Create users and review role boundaries.
- Enter a synthetic job by voice, screenshot, and manual form as applicable.
- Review and correct extracted details before saving.
- Assign the test job and walk through technician actions.
- Complete the test job and verify the configured invoice path.
- Record the baseline and pilot success targets.

### Adoption cadence

| Timing | Action | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Run one supervised real job | Owner and technician can complete the agreed workflow |
| Day 3 | Review failed or bypassed jobs | Each issue has an owner and workaround |
| Day 7 | Compare usage to pilot target | Continue, narrow scope, or stop based on evidence |
| Day 14 | Review scorecard and decision | Expand, extend with a reason, or close the pilot |

## Objection handling

**"We already use spreadsheets and text."**

"That may be enough. The useful comparison is one real job: how many times is it re-entered, who can see the latest version, and what has to happen before invoicing? If FieldBase does not reduce that burden in the pilot, we should not expand it."

**"We need WorkMarket or Field Nation."**

"Let's verify the exact connection and fields you require before we scope a pilot. I will not treat a connector as production-ready until we demonstrate it with your workflow."

**"AI gets details wrong."**

"The extracted job is a draft. The dispatcher reviews and edits it before saving. For the pilot we can measure correction frequency and keep manual entry available."

**"My technicians will not use another app."**

"Then the pilot should involve one lead technician and one job type. We will measure actual use by day three and stop or simplify if it adds work."

**"What about customer recordings?"**

"Voice job entry records only while the user holds the workflow open, sends the audio for transcription when configured, and keeps typed entry available. We should confirm your data policy and avoid recording customer conversations without proper authorization."

## Sales and onboarding scorecard

Track evidence, not impressions:

| Stage | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Qualified | Named pain, workflow owner, eligible pilot jobs, mandatory systems listed |
| Demo complete | Prospect workflow mapped; supported and unsupported requirements recorded |
| Pilot ready | Users named, data rules agreed, baseline and targets entered |
| Pilot healthy | Job IDs used, issues logged, day-three and day-seven checks completed |
| Expansion ready | Day-fourteen scorecard met and decision owner agrees |

For every loss or stalled pilot, record one primary reason: no priority, missing capability, integration gap, security/compliance requirement, adoption failure, price, or no decision owner. Feed repeated product gaps back into planning without turning them into sales promises.
